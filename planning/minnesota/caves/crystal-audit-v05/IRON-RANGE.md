# Iron Range cave audit

I reviewed the seven v04 rooms in full and in the native decision cameras. The [Crystal atlas](../crystal-reference.html), particularly Mt. Mortar, Slowpoke Well, and Rock Tunnel, shows how terrain can explain a journey before the player sees the next event. Mt. Mortar uses footholds, actual climbs, and water to change what a landing offers. Slowpoke Well changes from tightly staged encounters to a water crossing with separate destinations. Rock Tunnel puts the shortest journey against pockets that the player must deliberately enter and leave.

| Room | Where v04 falls short | Design target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Route 9 adit | The lower arrival steps into a broad square with several equivalent paths around small rocks. The foreman and the visible survey item share one straight shelf. Once the player reaches that shelf, the crossing toward Nell stays nearly straight even though the old map paints several separate shoulders. The two legal drops feel identical and give a return mainly because they are reversible by the nearby stair. | Define a single legible arrival bend, a distinct survey recess on the higher ground, and a shortcut landing the player can recognize from both directions. Keep the survey and later shaft separate. |
| Drill Approach | The ascent has two similarly framed open platforms. The first guard waits in a large room whose width does little, while the Ether is practically on the through route. Several quiet textured strips are legal floor, making the uneven stone edge hard to read. | Give the climb a sequence of narrow approach, usable passing shelf, and actual optional branch. Place the Ether beyond a separate turn, and make the weak camera show where the route continues. |
| Haulage | The rail alignments work, but the two large halls and their similarly spaced paired hatches obscure which workings they serve. The visual amount of continuous floor around the rig and in the runoff bay greatly exceeds the useful decision space. The shutter only comes into focus late. | Keep the functional sidings and make them explain distinct room shapes. Shape one purposeful turnout at the weigh controls and a staggered runoff room reached by passing the rail stop. Make the opening and flank visible from the practical crossing route. |
| Sump | The water reads as a straight artificial rectangle. The little rock holding the cache is visually lost in its middle, and the east service leg repeats the proportions of the dry west leg. The bypass alongside the water feels like a generic long walkway. | Let the connected water bend around its actual branch destination and reveal the rock from a usable bank. Keep the initial dry route available, while Surf reaches the cache and offers a physically different way around the flooded interior. |
| Ventilation | Much of the room repeats tall shafts with similar dark walls and empty floor. The grating is useful once the player is on it, but its approach is hidden behind a broad blank face and the fan looks like a lone prop in widening rock. The southern hatch landing gives little sense of what lies past it. | Give the fan its own shoulder and make the lower junction legible before the player reaches the valve. Let a compact maintenance turn distinguish the western and eastern landings. |
| Pump Gallery | The inspection loop finally bypasses the true shutter, but the lower route saves only four steps. Its two halves have far more open floor than the shutter reveal, trainer, or feed panel use. The open ground beyond the descent looks like staging for another leg. | Frame an upper inspection route and a plainly shorter lower link around the same solid buttress. Fit the feed panel into its own destination alcove and make the Core exit a narrower, separate reveal. |
| Drill Core | The main chamber draws a clear machine but still has a broad unused eastern lobe, near-empty crew approach, and blank apron below the machine. Almost every scene beat competes in the same open view instead of the cave progressively revealing the operation. | Give the crew approach, witness floor, rescue staging, and final control a distinct spatial rhythm. Remove the empty edge, pull existing solid stone closer around the approach, and respect all authored movement and machinery states. |

## Rebuilt routes

- **Route 9 adit** draws the lower landing into a hooked entrance and places the spare case at (5, 3) above a small rock shoulder, separate from the foreman. Both one-way jumps at (10, 8) and (11, 8) still have a 16-step uphill return. Reachable floor shrinks from 222 to 207 walking tiles.
- **Drill Approach** bends the ascent around a stepped shoulder. The player can detour to the Ether at (19, 18) and rejoin the climb. The shortest through trip is 38 steps. The shortest trip that includes an Ether interaction is 42 steps. Either upper landing approach stays available.
- **Haulage** brings the western bay around the weigh controls and folds the east floor toward the runoff. The existing tracks pass all 56 rail join checks, including the cart stop and the separated pressure shutter.
- **Sump** has a shoreline that bends away, comes in at the launch, then opens around the cache rock. The launch is at (7, 13). The dry route still works in 26 steps, and the Surf route needs 20. The waterline cache still requires Surf. The valve reads behind a narrower handturn.
- **Ventilation** has two continuous dry passages from the west landing to the grating junction. The fan passage curves around the rock rib while the other follows the west wall past the guard. Each takes 20 steps. The existing shutter, grating, valve, and eastern landings remain in use.
- **Pump Gallery** now sends the player down and around the stone buttress before climbing the upper inspection step. Feed isolation cuts the return from 48 to 30 steps, up from a four-step saving in v04. The Core shutter remains a separate gated destination.
- **Drill Core** removes the broad unused lower and eastern apron and steps the stone in around the separate crew entrance, view of the machinery, and control bend. Reachable floor falls from 359 to 241 tiles before the drill is stopped. The full scripted witness line, crew route, machine frames, and actor sightlines retain their movement. I reviewed both native day and nite palettes at player scale.

The geometry uses the original Minnesota artwork and one additional cave metatile composed of existing glyphs. It paints actual floor at six shoulders where walkable cells had blended into the quiet background. The layout carries forward the installed warp records and scripts. Both object relocations keep their original identifiers, event flags, and interaction scripts.

The [compiled mine audit](review_mine_audit.py) checks 32 progression and movement combinations, the drill's three terrain states, rail joins and stops, the two local actor mirrors, the optional Ether detour, the two ventilation passages, the Sump launch, and the longer open/closed Pump shortcut.
