Pokémon Crystal Cave Atlas

Pictures and floor-by-floor notes for every cave in the original Game Boy Color release. Crystal has 36 natural cave maps across 13 locations, plus the Burned Tower basement. The atlas also includes Blaine’s cave at Seafoam, the Dragon Shrine and the Ruins of Alph.

Open any picture at full size. The numbered markers and route arrows come from the atlas author. Some plates show dark rooms lit for reference, and some split one game map into several illustrated panels.

What this means for Minnesota

Johto

Dark Cave

Violet side
The Route 31 mouth opens beside a small Potion spur. A large L-shaped pool and attached rock divide the entrance pocket from the long, folded Route 46 arm. The Route 45 connection sits across the water on the other side of the room. Events
Blackthorn side
Route 45 enters from high ground. Downward ledges let the player travel toward the Violet side without opening an early passage back up to Route 45. The lake, BlackGlasses alcove and isolated item shelves make this a separate outing from the early entrance pocket. Events

Study how two rooms can have distinct travel directions even when they share the same palette. Keep the direction of a ledge legible at the landing.

Union Cave

1F
Routes 32 and 33 are at opposite ends of the long walk. Big shelves and ponds give the path its turns. Trainers and nearby supplies punctuate it, while two separate ladders peel away from the crossing. Events
B1F
Two separate sectors reward the two ladder choices. The northern sector offers Swift and approaches to the Ruins of Alph. The low southern water corridor connects to the deeper Lapras route. Events
B2F
A chain of banks and pools stages landings and trainer meetings on the way to the southern basin. Friday Lapras gives the trip a reason to happen again after the player learns Surf. Events

A required route can be easy to read on the first visit and still support two very different optional excursions.

Slowpoke Well

B1F
One raised shelf, a rock shoulder and the lower pool are enough to organize the Rocket rescue. The first visit uses the dry loop. The optional basement ladder is reached later with Strength and Surf. Events
B2F
The ladder lands on a small platform in the water. Two separate banks give clear reasons to disembark: the King’s Rock gift on one side and Rain Dance on the other. Events

A story cave can fit in a small room. A later descent works best when it changes what the player does.

Burned Tower basement

1F, tower context
The rival scene opens the center of the damaged wooden floor, turning the story event itself into a descent. Events
B1F
The fall lands south of a short stair approach to the beasts. The surrounding arms give the player another way out and space for later exploration. The east arm has a Strength boulder and the Endure TM, details omitted from the annotated plate. Events

Keep the encounter room and its arrival aimed at the focal point. Put the optional work somewhere it will not distract from that reveal.

Whirl Islands

Northwest
A tiny entry vestibule gives quick access to B1F. Other pockets in the same map link to the southwest side and a separate connector. Events
Northeast
A ledge choice changes which B1F landing the player reaches. The upper route puts the player on the approach to the waterfall descent. The other route offers an Ultra Ball. Events
Southwest
A pool and separate rock passages divide routes to different B1F landings and a lower outlet. Events
Southeast
A narrow spur framed by water has a single ladder into B1F, making it easier to recognize on the way back. Events
Connector
A short passage links a northwest pocket to B1F, keeping the route network folded without widening the main room. Events
B1F
The shared dry maze puts close-looking ladders on different heights and sides of rock spines. The stair and ledge orientation explains why the entrance choice matters. Events
B2F
Two entries from B1F land on different shelves. Water, the long waterfall drop and the separate southwest outlet give this floor a different navigation problem. Events
Lugia chamber
A dedicated pool gives the destination a much simpler shape than the route to it. The Silver Wing controls whether Lugia appears in Crystal. Events

Use a network only when different entrances matter. Give each arrival a feature the player can recognize from the small camera.

Mt. Mortar

1F entrance hall
Three Route 42 entrances meet different dry shelves around the central river. The waterfall points out a later route to 2F. The low middle entrance gives access to the basement side. Events
1F inner cave
This is a large dry room, 40 × 54 walking tiles. Rock spines, embedded stairs and a Strength pinch shape the lower west–east crossing and the deeper branches. Higher reward shelves help mark changes of direction. Events
2F
A walkable network of ledges and pockets wraps around several pools. Coming in above the waterfall changes both the approach and which destinations are close. Events
B1F
A winding flooded corridor separates the landings. Kiyo and the Tyrogue gift anchor the northwest arm and give the basement a destination beyond its item pockets. Events

A large cave earns its size by offering different kinds of trips across the same mountain.

Ice Path

1F
A short slide introduces the movement, the broad upper sheet makes the player chain turns, and a separate smaller puzzle holds HM07 Waterfall. The Blackthorn exit uses the far side of the same map. Events
B1F
Four pushable boulders and four holes make the player prepare the floor below from stable ground. A separate sector of the same map serves the return toward Blackthorn. Events
B2F, Mahogany side
The big ice sheet uses the dropped boulders as stopping points. The central ladder is the goal, while dry corners allow a player to recover their bearings. Events
B3F
A smaller stair chamber gives a break from long sliding sequences. The NeverMeltIce sits by a small Rock Smash detour. Events
B2F, Blackthorn side
A narrower ice room asks for a different angle of approach. Rest occupies a separate side landing before the ascent back through B1F and 1F. Events

Teach an action in a short room, combine it with another action in the main puzzle, and give each later slide a different approach or stopping point. Crystal’s ice art makes the sliding surface and the stable bank easy to distinguish.

Dragon’s Den

1F
Two small vestibules connect by stairs within one map. The tight rock approach holds back the reveal of the large lake. Events
B1F
Dry shelves carry trainers around the shore. Surf and a Whirlpool crossing take the player toward the shrine at the far side of the basin. The Dragon Fang has its own landing. Events
Dragon Shrine, indoor annex
The crossing ends in a dedicated story room. Crystal puts the elder’s quiz and the Rising Badge scene here, with a Dratini gift available afterward. Events

Use one landmark large enough to remember, then give its actual entrance a deliberate approach.

Mt. Silver

Room 1
The dark entrance zigzags through dry stairs, rock shelves and supply pockets. Flash changes how much of that structure the player can read. Events
Room 2
Waterfall channels break up the ledges. The onward route to Room 3 has a separate exit from the two branches that lead to reward chambers. Events
Item rooms
Two disconnected pockets share one map record. Their separate entrances and returns make them side destinations with distinct approaches. Events
Room 3
A long, narrow entrance opens into a warm, spare chamber with Red on a raised platform. The change of color and route width makes the last approach feel different without another puzzle. Events

A large encounter room can use empty space purposefully. The empty approach makes Red easier to see and gives the arrival time to land.

Kanto and the League approach

Tohjo Falls

Single room
Both Route 27 mouths are on the south edge of a 30 × 18 walking tile map. The shared channel curves around the central rock. The player climbs the west waterfall, crosses along the top and descends the east falls. The Moon Stone is tucked into a west bank recess. Events

One landform can make an entire route memorable. Place the field move where the shape of the room naturally asks the player to use it.

Victory Road

Lower tier
The Pokémon League gate lands the player at the base of a tall, dry cave. Rock shoulders and stairs bend the walk around a long raised shelf. Events
Middle tier
Raised pockets give the player a reason to explore. Two ladders to the upper tier and the landing from the optional Earthquake drop keep the return route meaningful.
Upper tier
One spur offers a one-way hole back to the Earthquake pocket. The rival intercepts the exit approach to Route 23. Most of the crossing is terrain and wild encounters, so that last fight gets room to stand out.

The three illustrated tiers are one 20 × 72 walking tile map with internal warps. A long dungeon can build anticipation with geography and one well-placed encounter.

Diglett’s Cave

Single map
Two tiny mouth lobbies and a long stair-step corridor connect Route 2 to the Vermilion side beside Route 11. Same-map ladders join the lobbies to the passage. One distinctive rock holds a hidden Max Revive, and Diglett and Dugtrio supply the cave’s encounter identity. Events

A connector can stay simple. A few bends, a visible feature and an encounter identity can carry it without a maze or a field move inside.

Mt. Moon

Single cave map
The Route 3 to Route 4 passage folds around a thick rock core with an early rival encounter. Two ladder pockets split off the passage and surface at the Square. All three cave pockets belong to one 30 × 18 walking tile map. Events
Mt. Moon Square, outdoor context
The outdoor terrace contrasts with the brief indoor passage. The shop and Monday-night Clefairy dance give the detour a reason of its own. Events

The most memorable part of a cave trip can be a place the cave takes you.

Rock Tunnel

1F
The Route 9 and south Route 10 mouths open into separate parts of 1F. Four ladder pairs carry the through route down to B1F, up for a middle stretch, down again and back up near the south mouth. The atlas calls the north exit Route 10, but its warp goes to Route 9. Steel Wing and hidden items give side shelves a reason to exist. Events
B1F
Rock masses divide the lower floor into two parts of the through route, with bent corridors between the ladder landings. Flash helps the player read those turns. Items reward a few true dead ends without turning them into false exits. Events

Where a cave is deliberately disorienting, distinguish the landing at each ladder and give side passages recognizable shapes.

Related underground rooms

Ruins of Alph

Four puzzle rooms
The Kabuto, Aerodactyl, Omanyte and Ho-Oh rooms use the same basic arrangement, with a different picture puzzle in each. Union Cave approaches are part of the larger route to the ruins. Events
Four item rooms and four word rooms
Separate word clues open optional back rooms. The item chambers and inscription chambers have a different purpose from solving the picture puzzles. Events
Shared inner chamber
Several descents land in the same long hall. Its rows of statues mark the shared destination and Unown habitat. Events

Room repetition can teach a rule when the player is meant to recognize the pattern and solve a different problem each time. These thirteen maps are classified as ruins dungeons.

Seafoam Islands Gym

Blaine’s room
Route 20 leads to one small cave-tile Gym, 10 × 8 walking tiles in the map data. The room is an immediate encounter with Blaine. Events

The original Crystal version uses Seafoam as a destination room. The older Seafoam dungeon and Cerulean Cave are outside the scope of this cartridge.

What this means for Minnesota

  • Choose what the cave does first. Diglett’s Cave connects two sides of the world, Slowpoke Well stages a rescue, Whirl Islands rewards choosing the right mouth, and Ice Path teaches a movement rule. Driftless Hollow and Rock Cut can stay direct, while Voyageurs Ice and the Iron Range can sustain layered decisions.
  • Draw the rock and water that control movement. Read the silhouettes of Dark Cave, Union Cave and Tohjo Falls. Each has a few large continuous masses, then rocks clustered at shoulders and recesses. In a 10 × 9 walking tile camera, a stair cut into a wall and the landing beyond it communicate more than several freestanding stones. Gunflint Grotto’s spring bank and Winding Cleft’s flooded fissure can carry that role in Minnesota.
  • Make each elevation change earn its place. Ice Path changes from sliding to boulder setup and back to sliding with new stops. Mt. Mortar changes from a dry crossing to flooded branches. For Voyageurs, evaluate every launch bank, stopping tile, dry recovery area and return. For the mine, make the lower and upper routes lead to different destinations or reveal a meaningful connection.
  • Give the return trip a purpose. Slowpoke Well and Union Cave put later rewards behind a change in available movement. Victory Road uses a one-way drop for an optional item. Check the route toward each reward, the landing and the way back independently, with special attention to asymmetric routes and doors that appear close on a full-map image.
  • Save wide space for a basin or a reveal. Dragon’s Den needs a lake wide enough to make the shrine a destination. Mt. Silver gives Red an empty approach after two busy rooms. In the smaller Minnesota shelters, a few tight turns and one purposeful turnout can do more work per screen. At the native camera, look for the first visible cue to the next decision, the largest empty stretch and how the arrival reads in reverse.

Sources and scope

The pictures are Daniel Chaviers’s Crystal map sheets, assembled from the original game and hosted on VGMaps. The 37 cave maps use Crystal’s CAVE category and exclude Ilex Forest and the unused Safari Zone map. Including Seafoam Gym, which uses the cave tileset and is classified as an indoor room, brings the total to 38 cave interiors. Dimensions are twice the metatile counts on each axis, expressed here in 16-pixel walking tiles. Individual warp and event scripts govern details where the pictures simplify the route or omit an object.
