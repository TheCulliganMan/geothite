# Woolwind family research

[Minnesota DNR's Logging Camps: The Early Years](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/forestry/history/logging-camps.html)
describes wool work clothing, including hand coverings, and the difficulty of
drying wet wool overnight in the bunkhouse.
[Minnesota Historical Society's Logging Industry](https://www.mnhs.org/foresthistory/learn/logging)
describes garments suspended overhead to dry in logging-camp bunkhouses.

Those documented materials and drying arrangements ground the design. The
living mitten, self-sewing sleeve wings and flying quilt are original fantasy,
not a claim about historic ghost stories or an interpretation of a sacred being.
The three stages change their body construction: a hooked mitten/cuff, two
lifted sleeve wings and a hood, then a broad three-panel quilt with a rolled
collar. No animal, person, clothesline or separate garment sits underneath or
outside the creature.

Ghost → Ghost/Flying → Ghost/Flying replaces the Water/Fighting seiche line.
Solid stitched wool, broad patch panels and mitten hooks distinguish this
family from the existing prairie-smoke plume designs.
