# Independent regional design audit B

Signed by Codex independent reviewer B on November 18, 2026.

I examined the installed selection named by Minnesota’s world manifest, `minnesota-story-flow-v01.crystalpack`, SHA-256 `02e18ba71203d8aa2e10658c6babc53512daeef592ee53fb2900630f91aa57cb`. I inspected the encounter records on all 355 Minnesota maps, rendered the 20 numbered-route cache host maps and 16 cave, mine and ice maps, and examined native-size terrain and camera crops. My controls were the original Crystal maps included in that same pack. Terrain pictures use installed foreground art and daytime colors. They omit actors. The measurements below use the installed actor/event data. No game session was run.

## Trainer battles

**1. Restore legible encounter lanes on numbered routes.** All 125 ordinary trainer records on the numbered outdoor chunks specify a standing direction and a sight distance of one to four tiles. None has a sight-direction override. The production movement scheduler turns every one of them into a fast random spinner and the sight check gives each a minimum range of eight tiles. This discards the geographic distinction between the angler watching a short pier, a sentry covering a crossing and a lookout scanning a clearing. Crystal’s Route 32 has seven fixed trainers and one fast-turning Youngster, with sight of one or three tiles. Preserve automatic sight battles and the existing once-defeated dialogue. Make starting direction, intended range and any rotation explicit for each lane. A reliable test should distinguish crossing the chosen lane from approaching along the safe bank. [Crystal Route 32 events](https://github.com/pret/pokecrystal/blob/master/maps/Route32.asm)

**2. Reorient nine trainers before changing that behavior.** Eight outdoor trainers initially face an adjacent wall and one faces out of bounds. The wall cases are R02 T02/T03, R05 T04, R07 T08, R10 T03, R12 T02 and R20 T05/T09. R12 T08 stands at (25,47), the final row of its chunk, facing south. The other 116 look immediately onto floor or grass. This is an initial geographic check. The current spinner can rescue these placements by turning. Move or turn each toward a real walkable, connected approach and confirm there is a square from which the player can see the trainer before crossing its lane. Verify ordinary trainers remain automatic and gym leaders remain conversation-initiated.

## Routes and native tiles

**3. Give the Route 15 lake cabin an approach a player can recognize from the trail.** The eastern cabin has a complete miniature front and blue roof. It sits flush against the woods, opening onto a broad patch of the same light lawn that fills the rest of the clearing. The forest wall between the house and lake is nearly continuous. On the full map the cabin reads as a generic house at the end of another clearing. Make the north–south foot trail bend around a visible water-facing landing, thin one short section of trees to expose the shoreline and put the road walker at that decision. Keep the pedestrian shoreline and cabin door continuously reachable. Crystal Route 32's shore puts fishers out over the water while its main trail continues south. The two destinations are recognizable before either interaction. Route 15 terrain (`b-figures/MnR15C00R00.png`) · Crystal comparison (`b-figures/Route32.png`)

**4. Fix the conspicuous summer tile in the Gunflint snow.** Route 12 uses the Gunflint winter tileset. Block (7,8) is ID 0x0f, with an emerald rectangular grass base touching snow on all sides beneath a pair of trees. Its playable Cut-tree behavior should survive the art correction. Adapt the ground-colored pixels for the native winter tile and review the cut state as well as the standing tree. If the same tile has summer uses, scope the changed art to the winter tileset. The missing transition is visible at ordinary camera scale. Native detail, upper left (`b-figures/tile-details.png`)

## Caves and mazes

**5. Make the Gunflint Grotto mouth conspicuous on the return trip.** The single exit is at (17,23). Its native block 0x24 is tucked directly between solid brown fill blocks 0x2e, with more fill beneath it. The yellow-floored passage visually runs straight through to the surrounding wall texture. The same exit block in Union Cave gains shape from its neighboring cliff side and stepped entrance apron. Frame the grotto mouth with contiguous cliff edges and a distinct floor approach so a player leaving the lake can recognize the exit from one camera away. Keep its existing coordinate, spring and item access. Grotto and Union Cave detail, lower row (`b-figures/tile-details.png`) · Full grotto (`b-figures/MnR12Cave1F.png`) · [Crystal Union Cave events](https://github.com/pret/pokecrystal/blob/master/maps/UnionCave1F.asm)

**6. Keep Voyageurs’ actual sliding puzzle and give its two chambers different jobs.** A collision-based slide graph starts 1F at its dry arrival (5,19). A shortest route to the B1F ladder (23,5) takes 12 direction choices, three of which cross ice. With ice blocked there is no route to that ladder or the visible healing item. On B1F the NeverMeltIce approach takes nine choices, including three ice crossings. The second-floor Revive also has a 17-step dry approach. This is substantive puzzle construction; the art makes the parallel long white-blue channels less distinct than the mechanics are. Make the optional B1F approach visibly wrap around the reward before its final slide and retain the dry Revive as a recovery/reorientation point. Crystal’s Ice Path visibly separates the optional Waterfall item from the ladder destination on 1F. Its longer travel is not a length target. ## Hidden items

**7. Teach a discovery cue early and then vary how players read the environment.** The 20 selected caches are installed as real hidden-item background events. Seven use local block 0x04. Their native art shows a flower patch on R01, R03, R08, R10 and R18, and tracked snow on R14 and R20. A shared early cue is learnable. Later repetitions lose specificity. Preserve the R01 teaching patch. Make one later lake reward sit at the end of a shore current or wrack line, and put one winter reward at the end of a track that changes direction. Let the cue point to one plausible tile and make its approach geographically distinct. The R04 bench, R02 paired hitching posts, and R13 isolated survey sign are strong existing examples. Crystal Route 32 hides its two items on different surfaces at (12,67) and (11,40). Twenty item cameras (`b-figures/cache-cameras.png`) · [Crystal Route 32 events](https://github.com/pret/pokecrystal/blob/master/maps/Route32.asm)

**8. Correct Route 11’s landmark-to-art mismatch.** The authored landmark calls the Ultra Ball spot “the short line of shore posts at the end of the headland spur.” At (50,18), block 0x82 depicts short horizontal steps beside a rock at the shore cut. No line of upright posts identifies the item in its camera. Route 16 provides a clear visual example of actual fence posts. Keep Route 11's independent headland reward and redraw or relocate its cue to agree with the actual feature. The source description is an authoring note. I did not verify that it is spoken in play. ## Coverage and limits

The route atlas (`b-figures/route-atlas.png`), cave atlas (`b-figures/cave-atlas.png`) and Crystal atlas (`b-figures/crystal.png`) are scaled overviews. The detailed PNGs preserve the original native pixels. Cache crops extending past a map edge show black outside that extracted map. I used each full map to distinguish a real edge from a crop boundary. The cave atlas covers the 16 rooms selected by cave, mine and ice naming, and the route atlas covers one cache host chunk per numbered route. The slide counts are static graph results with initial actors occupying their tiles, native ice motion and directional ledge restrictions. They do not demonstrate handling or battle pacing in live play. Reading the routes as dull or the landmarks as ambiguous is my design judgment.
