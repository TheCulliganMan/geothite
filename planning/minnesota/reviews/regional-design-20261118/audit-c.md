# C · Minnesota regional design audit

**Reviewed build** · Locally selected `minnesota-story-flow-v01.crystalpack`, SHA-256 `02e18ba71203d8aa2e10658c6babc53512daeef592ee53fb2900630f91aa57cb`. The hash matched the selection in `planning/minnesota/world.json` on November 18, 2026.

**Coverage** · I inspected the installed native terrain of all eight numbered cave first floors, both Voyageurs ice floors, the State Fairgrounds, and 20 outdoor partitions across Routes 6, 8, 9, 13, 17 and 20. I rendered the interaction square of each of the 20 numbered outdoor route caches and inspected the sight settings of all 301 Minnesota trainer objects. Five maps received additional sprite overlays at authored positions. This is a static examination of artwork, coordinates, collision and runtime rules. I did not play or measure player behavior. The joins of the complete regional campaign and all gym floors are outside this submission's visual sample.

## 1. Make the trainer's stance informative

All 221 Minnesota trainers without a direction override have authored ranges from one to five. The runtime raises each to eight. On Minnesota maps it also converts authored standing poses and slow spinners to fast spinners for these trainers. A radius of one beside a picnic table and a range of five at an overlook can therefore produce the same rotating eight-square challenge. The other 80 trainers keep explicitly fixed direction and shorter authored range. I traced those 80 rays at their initial coordinates and found no ordinary floor behind an intervening wall, water, tree or unconditionally standing actor. There is no demonstrated through-wall battle in this sample.

Let range, facing and turning describe the room. Retain automatic challenge on sight and the clear-path check. Give the first route a visible cross-lane sentry, a bank-side angler looking along the shore, and an optional spinner guarding a reward. Confirm the sentry sees a reachable square before restricting its movement. On Crystal Route 32 the angler Justin faces down at range one, Ralph faces up at range three, and Gordon spins at range three. The player can learn these differences from their positions. ## 2. Give the State Fair a parade avenue and genuine queues

The fair has 255 actors, including 137 maze guests and 35 sight trainers. One queue puts eight guests at x=20,23,26,29,32,35,38,41 on y=4. Other rows repeat the same spacing down unrelated aisles. In the actor-inclusive native image, the repeated rows look like fences that wander across the middle of wide grounds. The barns and stands are recognizable at close range. The relation between their entrances is much harder to see. There are seven signs and six warp records serving three interiors.

Choose the grandstand as the visual anchor. Run an identifiable parade avenue past its entrance. Bend two or three queues into compact pockets in front of actual counters, ending each queue at a server. Give at least one side lane an unobstructed rejoin to the parade avenue. Keep busy people around real attractions. Crystal's National Park makes a very different social space feel legible by building the entire circuit around its center, then placing its characters in relation to that circuit. The Quick Claw teacher and her Persian make one of these little groups memorable. ## 3. Let one fair detour pay off in the environment

The entire fair exterior has seven readable signs and zero hidden item events. A busy side activity can have plenty of other rewards, but the outdoor map offers no reward for recognizing an odd paving stone or slipped item behind a stall. Put one fair-appropriate existing consumable by the last bench overlooking the show ring. Make the back route visible through the rails from the avenue and reveal its short entrance near the barn. Crystal's National Park has a hidden Full Heal as well as visible item balls and the teacher's gift. [National Park map events](https://github.com/pret/pokecrystal/blob/master/maps/NationalPark.asm#L488-L522)

## 4. Give both sides of Granite Den a payoff

The Route 17 den has a real compact shelf circuit, a local observer, a visible pickup at (17,5), and a hidden Spell Tag in the boulder at (17,7). The two goods can be reached from (18,5) and (18,7), only two floor steps apart on the eastern arm. The top-side room has room for a choice, but both rewards favor the same spur. Move the visible pickup to the western lichen alcove, far enough from the entrance that its first glimpse invites a short climb. Leave the hidden reward on the distinctive rock and keep the den's quiet tone. Link the observer's existing lichen story to the exposed shelf with one piece of native light-colored stone art. Union Cave demonstrates how to spread four visible pickups and the entrances to lower-floor branches along a changing shoreline. A one-room den only needs one such choice. ## 5. Let Route 17 reveal the landform before the player reaches it

The regional route has an attractive glacial patch and a lake in its middle. From the northern approach, 637 of 768 metatiles use the same evergreen block. Its southernmost partition uses a single woodland block for 229 of its 256 metatiles. The trail visibly contracts to a narrow seam near the southern end. Large forest margins make sense. Along the playable edge, however, ordinary spruce walls often carry both the travel lane and the decision to turn. Cut a small granite sightline across the inside bend leading to the den, with the cave or a pale outcrop visible before the branch. Mirror that exposed stone on the cave entry wall. Preserve the broad snow-to-grass transition on Route 8, which already lets the terrain tell the player that they are going somewhere different. Route comparison sheet (`c-evidence/routes-sheet.png`)

## 6. Distinguish particular discoveries within the hidden-item vocabulary

The installed coordinates of all 20 numbered route caches match their route definitions. The picnic bench on Route 4, iron cap on Route 9 and survey marker on Route 13 provide legible native cues. Routes 1, 3, 8, 10 and 18 each put the item in a similar bright flower patch. Routes 14 and 20 use the same small footprint motif between snowy trees. Familiar cues help teach searching, but five variants of the same flower cluster flatten a riverbank, a snowline and a lakeside into the same discovery.

Keep the early flower lesson on Route 1. Make the Route 10 snowline clue a single flower growing beside the last snowdrift, visibly distinct from the larger flower patch on Route 8. On Route 18 use a shoreline object that can be seen across water before the player finds the land approach. Keep the current item identities and collection flags. Inspect each final image at character scale, with the exact interactable quadrant identified. ## 7. Give the ordinary cave rooms a distinct first impression

The Route 3, 13 and 20 first floors each contain a compact, almost rectangular pool, purple floor and rounded outcrops. Their block layouts are different, but the little bodies of water make the rooms rhyme at the first glance. Use one stronger relationship per room. In Route 3, let a stream entering the back wall explain the riverbank entrance. In Route 13, pull one shelter shelf forward to frame the water behind it. Preserve Route 20's two-exit crossing and make its seep a narrow bed that follows the lower route. These are visual design proposals. The audit did not find that these floors were impossible to traverse.

Voyageurs is a counterexample worth protecting. The cleft between the two icefields on 1F and the inward-turning central shelf on B1F produce two recognizable silhouettes from the same tiles. Route 12 also has a visibly distinct pool with an island. These layouts show how far an authored landform can carry navigation without extra text or trainers.
