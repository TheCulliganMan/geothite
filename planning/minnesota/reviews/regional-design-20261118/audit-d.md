## First priority — make trainer positions produce a decision

Nine trainers face solid wall for every tile of their configured sight range. Two sit on Route 2, the player’s early opportunity to learn what a watching trainer means. At (23,19) the trainer looks west into a shrub. At (47,37) the second looks east into a wall. On Route 12 the trainer at (51,29) faces two tiles of pine canopy. Talking from the side may still work. Reposition or turn these nine toward a usable approach, then check whether an ordinary player can see the actor before entering its sightline. The other affected trainers are R05 T04, R07 T08, R10 T03, R12 T08, and R20 T05 and T09. All placements (`d-evidence/route-trainers.csv`) · Camera crops, upper row (`d-evidence/camera.png`)

All 125 Minnesota trainers in this census stand facing a fixed direction. Eighty-four watch just one tile. In the Johto census, 28 of 84 trainers rotate and 19 watch one tile. Crystal’s Route 32 shows why both kinds belong. Stationary fishers occupy their stretch of water while a turning Youngster watches the inland track. Choose a handful of bends where players can wait for a turn or risk the grass. Keep narrow stationary watchers where their occupation or a tight bridge gives that direction meaning. ## Best through-route — Pine Country, Route 6

The river bridge, lookout, fishing deck and rod-giver’s house are distinct enough to remember on the return trip. The angler connects the deck and house through their own experience. The south and north signs name the towns on either end. More importantly, the through-route offers a measurable choice. I joined the two chunks in their native orientation and compared northbound paths using only FLOOR and TALL_GRASS, treating warps and actor bodies as blocked. The lowest grass count crosses 14 grass tiles and one watched tile in 154 steps. Avoiding every sightline crosses 20 grass tiles in 166 steps. This is a static comparison of those tile types. ## Hidden items — teach players which details to inspect

The 53 Minnesota hidden-item background events span 46 maps. Thirty-six sit on FLOOR, 16 on WALL and one on BUOY. Collision alone says little about the clue. In a player-size view the Route 6 Parlyz Heal at (8,30) is an ordinary patch beside trees. By contrast, the Voyageurs basement PP Up occupies the solitary boulder in a blind alcove at (27,6). Both are optional. Only the latter makes the exact search spot legible. Move the Route 6 item onto an inspectable stump or place a distinctive feature at the trail pocket. Leave some well-marked pockets empty so searching stays a choice. Camera crops, lower row (`d-evidence/camera.png`) · Hidden items and adjacent terrain (`d-evidence/hidden-items.csv`)

## Caves and mazes — give the return trip consequences

Voyageurs makes ice readable. The striped sliding surface, bare footing and rock stops have distinct silhouettes. On B1F, the Max Revive to the northwest has a 17-step walking route along the western margin. A conservative static slide model also finds a way to the NeverMeltIce and the hidden PP Up across ice. Players can choose between an easy reward and learning the stops. Both basement warps return to the same upper landing. A late optional chute back to the entrance, or a visible change made upstairs by a basement action, would give the second floor a consequence beyond collecting items. Crystal’s Ice Path B1F changes the floor below by dropping four boulders through marked holes. Ice floors side by side (`d-evidence/ice.png`) · [Crystal’s boulder and hole events](https://github.com/pret/pokecrystal/blob/master/maps/IcePathB1F.asm)

The route caves on 3, 10, 12, 13 and 17 each have exactly one outward warp and no other warp. Route 12 earns its single mouth. Its pool and island dominate the room, the angler talks about swimmers, and Otta offers to heal the party. Route 10 is the better place to try a second exit. Its shelves and eastern water basin already organize a loop, but after three trainer placements and four field pickups the player leaves where they entered. Connect one upper shelf to a distinct place on the exterior with a visible payoff. Minnesota’s own Route 5 Rock Cut and Route 20 cave already connect different chunks. Crystal’s Union Cave gives its main floor two exterior destinations and two basement stairways. Route 10, Route 12, Union Cave and Mt. Mortar (`d-evidence/caves.png`)
