# The first three Fen Waterline badge battles

**Keep all three first-battle teams.** The six ordinary trainers and three leaders have identical battle records in the selected Intro edition and its Field sibling. Both enable competitive battle decisions and keep campaign trainer levels fixed. Wild levels listed below are encounter-table floors and can rise with the player's party. This review checked the compiled records and battle code. Win rates and time spent training were outside its scope. | Badge | What the fight asks | An answer available on the journey |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rochester | Tavel's Nibbin introduces Quick Attack. Varel's Lv. 10 Nibbin can lower Defense with Tail Whip and hit with 70-power physical Headbutt. The Lv. 12 Crookit adds priority and Dark-type Bite. | Each starter learns a damaging elemental move at Lv. 5 that hits both for neutral damage. Route 1 also offers Driptip in all three time periods. Its Rock typing halves the Normal attacks. Crookit's Bite still hits it for neutral damage. |
| Mankato | Torven's Lv. 14 Sodcalf introduces Mud-Slap's accuracy drop. Lv. 15 Burrbud can use Growth and recover HP with Mega Drain. Jorel, a required trainer, has already shown the Lv. 16 ace Mudnip's two-turn Dig. | Route 1 Pinit learns Absorb at 5 and Ember at 9. Ember hits Burrbud for double damage, and Absorb hits Water/Ground Mudnip for quadruple damage. Route 2 also offers Vanechick in the night grass table. It is immune to Dig, resists Bite, and learns Wing Attack at 13. |
| Minneapolis | Avrin opens with Lv. 19 Pipulse's physical ThunderPunch. Lv. 20 Sparkveil can set Light Screen, and Lv. 22 Electric/Grass Tapacitor can punish an Electric-immune Water/Ground partner with Mega Drain. Jossa introduces the Grass attack on Zapsap first. | Burrowbill occupies the 30% second slot in the morning and daytime grass tables inside Route 3's River Pocket Cave. Its Ground/Flying typing blocks Electric and takes neutral Grass damage. It learns Mud-Slap at 9 for the two pure Electric opponents. Against the ace, a Fire attack such as Pinit's Ember hits for double damage. |

The packed Quick Attack effect has priority 2 against ordinary moves at 1. The turn resolver interprets Mud-Slap's native effect as a one-stage Accuracy loss, lets Dig's user evade most incoming attacks while underground, and applies Light Screen to special damage. Consequently Light Screen can reduce Mud-Slap even though Mud-Slap is super effective against Sparkveil. Varel's disruption profile weighs persistent status, Encore, Disable and attraction. His listed team has none of those effects, so the interesting decisions here come from the moves themselves. Crystal's Falkner shows Mud-Slap on two birds, Bugsy builds to a Scyther with priority and Fury Cutter, and Whitney gives Clefairy and Miltank distinct jobs. Crystal Legacy keeps Falkner at two Pokémon and adds an early Quick Attack. Minnesota already uses these ideas in different fights. The strongest reason to keep this lineup is that the nearby catches answer particular opponents without neutralizing the entire next battle.
