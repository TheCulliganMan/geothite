# Ely Hall of Fame

The recorder promises to write down every partner who finished the League run. A family visitor remembers how her father lifted his Pokémon to see the book. The former room put both characters on the same speckled floor in front of an electronic terminal. The new room places an open register beside the recorder on a burgundy carpet. Above them, an oak-framed transom shows the northern lakeshore. The carpet’s woven star is visible from the Champion’s doorway.

An east gallery gives the family photograph its own destination. A bench faces the portrait of a trainer holding up a Jiglet. Examine the picture to see Jiglet at full portrait scale. A button press reveals the child's caption. The original visitor still tells the family story from her place off the central aisle. Returning to the register requires no puzzle or extra conversation.

The Hall has a private Game Boy Color tile bank. Each 8-by-8 tile uses two bitplanes and picks one of eight four-color RGB555 palettes. Archive volumes beneath the window and the walnut bench use the original house tiles. The runner, star, lake scene, book and family portrait use newly drawn tiles. The photograph opens Jiglet's existing 56-by-56 monochrome portrait.

The published Prism display-case screenshot separates the things a visitor can inspect from their approach lanes. Polished Crystal’s Pewter Museum lets a visitor enlarge a small fossil display. Its script holds the illustration until the player chooses to turn back to the caption. Those references led to the recessed east gallery, a dedicated viewing position and the optional close-up. Minnesota supplies the room design, furnishings, family image and Pokémon artwork.
