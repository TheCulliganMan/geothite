# Reading Minnesota terrain on foot

**Gunflint Trail.** The loose rock sits at the base of a fracture. Short, angular fragments continue down from the cliff face. Opening the cleft gives the player a choice between crossing the frozen basin and walking the ridge. The exposed iron-formation around Gunflint Lake supplied a local reference for the layered cliff. [Minnesota Geological Survey](https://cse.umn.edu/mgs/ancient-meteorite-deposit-minnesota)

**Mille Lacs Shore.** Behind the sapling, a broad gray erratic rests in grass. Its rounded upper face and mossy foot distinguish it from the angular pieces beneath Gunflint’s cliff. The east side is within reach from the clearing and holds a Hard Stone. The Mille Lacs Uplands include end moraines and deposits of glacial till. [Minnesota Department of Natural Resources](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/ecs/212Kb/index.html)

**Iron Range and the river park.** Inside the mine, one breakable fracture opens a survey gallery. The public picnic path crosses ordinary ground in both directions. Crystal’s Dark Cave provided a reference for the mine’s rock interaction. The Minnesota routes use their own terrain to show where that interaction belongs. [Pokémon Crystal’s Dark Cave events](https://github.com/pret/pokecrystal/blob/master/maps/DarkCaveVioletEntrance.asm)
